The 30-Day Reset

You're not broken. You're miscalibrated. Thirty days, three rules, one plan for the exact second a craving hits. Read it once tonight, then take it one day at a time.

The three rules

  1. 1

    No porn. That's the reset. Everything else in this guide exists to make this one rule easier to keep.

  2. 2

    Phone sleeps outside your bedroom. Most slips happen late at night, in bed, alone with a screen. Remove the setting and you remove most of the fights.

  3. 3

    Replace, don't just remove. Every time you'd normally reach for it, do something physical instead: pushups, a walk, a cold shower.

Week by week

Days 1–7

Clear the ground

  • Install a blocker on every device you own. Yes, the tablet too.
  • Write down three reasons you're doing this. Keep them on your lock screen.
  • Unfollow accounts that feed the habit. You know which ones.
  • Get outside in daylight within an hour of waking up.

What to expect: cravings are loudest this week, usually at night and when you're bored or stressed. That's normal, and it passes.

Days 8–14

Fix your sleep

  • Same bedtime every night, 7–9 hours. One week of 5-hour nights has been shown to drop testosterone by 10–15%.
  • Screens off 30 minutes before bed. Read, stretch, or just lie there.
  • Train at least 3 times this week. Any workout counts.

What to expect: some guys feel flat or unmotivated around now. Keep going. It's a phase, not your new normal.

Days 15–21

Take back your time

  • Check your screen time. Cut your worst app by half.
  • Pick one hard thing to learn or build and give it 30 minutes a day.
  • See a friend in person. Isolation is fuel for the habit.

What to expect: more energy and focus start showing up. This is also when overconfidence causes slips. Keep the blockers on.

Days 22–30

Lock it in

  • Write down what's changed since day 1. Read it on bad days.
  • Plan your next 30. The reset is the start, not the finish line.
  • Tell one person you trust what you're doing.

What to expect: it stops feeling like a fight most days. You're not in the audience seat anymore.

When a craving hits

Cravings peak and fade, usually within 10–20 minutes. You don't have to beat it forever. You have to outlast the next 10 minutes.

  1. Name it. Say "this is a craving, it will pass."
  2. Move. Stand up and leave the room you're in.
  3. Shock the system. 20 pushups or cold water on your face.
  4. Read your three reasons.
  5. Set a 10-minute timer. If you still want to after, do it again.

Track your 30

Tap a day when you finish it clean. 0 / 30 done. Saved on this device only.

If you slip

Don't spiral, and don't wait until Monday to restart. One slip doesn't erase the days before it. Write down what triggered it (time, place, mood), fix that one thing, and start day 1 again tonight.
