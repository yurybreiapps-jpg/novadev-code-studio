First Swing Guide

Use this checklist for the first clip. Clean video gives the AI better evidence and makes progress easier to compare later.

  1. Record from the side. Side view is best for contact point, racket path, preparation, balance, and follow-through. Behind the baseline is also useful for court position and recovery.
  2. Keep the full player visible. Shoes, shoulders, racket, ball contact, and finish should stay inside the frame.
  3. Trim the best segment. Select the clearest 20 to 90 seconds depending on your access level and analysis mode.
  4. Choose technique or Auto Detect. Pick Forehand, Backhand, Serve, Volley, or another technique when you know it. Use Auto Detect for mixed clips.
  5. Practice one Fix of the Day. Focus on one correction, then upload another clip from the same angle next week.

How it works

AI swing analysis

A-Tennis reviews selected key frames and clip metadata to estimate mechanics such as preparation, timing, contact point, kinetic chain, balance, and follow-through.

Visual checkpoints

Reports include timestamps and visual checkpoints so feedback feels tied to the actual swing, not just generic text.

Training progress

Saved analyses, player profiles, personal bests, trends, and action plans help players see what to practice next.

AI, privacy, and credits

What is sent

For AI analysis, the app sends selected key frames from your chosen clip range, clip timing, technique, player level, and analysis mode to the A-Tennis backend for Google Gemini AI analysis.

What stays local

The original imported video, local trimmed clip, player profiles, saved history, and generated reports are stored on the device unless you share or export them.

FAQ

What clip angle is best?

Start with side view. It gives the clearest look at contact point, racket drop, timing, and follow-through.

Can I analyze friends or family?

Yes. Use player profiles to keep histories and training plans separate. Make sure people agree to be recorded.
