The honest answer first: for most people the differences that matter are not the features, they are what it costs and what it does with your data. Here is what the well-known ones actually charge, what their privacy labels say, and where PhotoMuse is — and is not — the right pick.

What their labels say about tracking.

Apple makes every developer declare this, and you can check it yourself: open any App Store page and scroll to App Privacy. On 17 September 2026 we installed the three best-known photo cleaners and read theirs.

Cleanup, Swipewipe and Clever Cleaner all declare “Data Used to Track You”, and all three show the tracking permission prompt when you open them.

PhotoMuse declares “Data Not Collected” and never shows that prompt, because there is nothing behind it — no account, no analytics, no advertising, and no server of ours for a photo to go to. Everything it does happens on your device.

This is not a claim you have to take our word for. It is on both App Store pages, in the same place, written in Apple's words rather than ours.

Fair questions.

I deleted thousands of photos and my storage did not change. Why?

Because deleting a photo — in any app, including Apple's own — moves it to Recently Deleted, where it sits for 30 days and still takes up room. Your storage does not change until that album is emptied. Open Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted → Select → Delete All. This is the single most common reason people think a cleaner app did nothing, and most of them never mention it.

Is PhotoMuse a Clever Cleaner alternative?

Partly, and it depends what you wanted. If the appeal is the free daily allowance, Clever Cleaner gives 5 GB a day and we do not match that. If what you wanted was an app that also works properly on an iPad, makes the photos you keep look better, and collects nothing about you, then yes.

Does compressing a video always make it smaller?

No, and this surprised us too. Re-encoding a video that is already efficient can make it bigger — in our own measurements a 55 MB clip came back at 124 MB. PhotoMuse asks Apple's encoder for an estimate before offering a video at all, and throws the result away if it did not actually shrink. HDR video stays HDR.

Does enhancing a photo make the file bigger?

It can, and most apps let it. Saving an iPhone HEIC photo as a JPEG at the same quality came back more than twice the size in our tests. PhotoMuse keeps each photo in the format it already had, so an app meant to free space does not quietly fill it.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Finding duplicates, judging bad shots, search, Enhance and compression all run on your iPhone or iPad. There is no server on our side. The one time data moves is when a photo's full-size original lives in your iCloud and Apple fetches it to your device so the work can be done — that is between you and Apple, and we never see it.

What is free?

Scanning, every pile, search, the whole month-by-month swipe review, and deleting photos you pick by hand — with no limit. Also free to try: cleaning 5 similar or duplicate groups, enhancing 5 photos and compressing 2 videos. Plus is for doing it in bulk.
