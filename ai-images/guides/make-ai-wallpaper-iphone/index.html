How to make a custom AI wallpaper for your iPhone

Wallpapers are the easiest win in AI image generation, because the requirements are unusually forgiving: no faces, no text, no anatomy to get wrong. Almost anything the model is good at makes a decent wallpaper.

Think about where the clock sits

This is the thing that separates a wallpaper that works from a picture that happens to be on your phone. The top third of your screen holds the clock and notifications; the bottom third holds the dock and unlock hint. Your image needs to be quiet in both.

So prompt for compositions with a calm top and bottom: a horizon low in the frame, a subject centred with open sky above, a gradient, a texture. Avoid busy detail edge to edge — it will fight everything the system draws on top.

Prompts that reliably work

  • Landscapes with space. Misty mountains at dawn, a lone tree on a hill, a still lake under cloud.
  • Abstract and texture. Flowing liquid colour, marbled stone, brushed metal, layered paper.
  • Night skies. Aurora, a starfield, a city seen from far away.
  • Single objects on plain grounds. One flower, one leaf, one geometric shape.

What to avoid: crowds, text, anything with hands, and dense patterns that make icons hard to read.

Aspect ratio, honestly

Most on-device generators draw square images, because that is what the model was trained on. A phone screen is roughly twice as tall as it is wide. You have two options and one of them is better.

The better one: generate square, then crop to the part you want. You lose some of the image but keep all of its quality. The worse one: stretch it, which distorts everything and always looks it.

Prompt with the crop in mind — put your subject where it will survive being cut down to a tall rectangle.

Make it bigger before you set it

A 768-pixel square looks fine on screen until you crop into it, at which point you are asking a small image to fill a large display. Upscaling before you crop gives the phone more pixels to work with, and the difference is visible on an OLED screen.

Setting it

Save to Photos, open the image, share, then Use as Wallpaper. iOS will let you pan and scale — this is where a taller crop pays off. Check it against both the lock screen and the home screen before you commit; images that look striking on the lock screen sometimes make app icons unreadable at home.

Depth effect

iOS can lift a foreground subject in front of the clock on the lock screen. It works best with a clear single subject against a distinctly separate background — which is another reason to prompt for one object rather than a busy scene.
