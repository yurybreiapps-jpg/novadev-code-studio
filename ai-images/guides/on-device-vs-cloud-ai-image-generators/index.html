On-device or cloud AI image generation: which should you use?

These are not competing versions of the same thing. They are different trades, and which one is right depends on facts about you rather than facts about the technology.

← All guides The app

Use cloud if…

  • You want the best possible image. Server models are larger than anything that fits on a phone, and it shows in prompt-following and in fine detail.
  • You need text inside the picture. Cloud models render lettering far more reliably. On-device models are poor at it.
  • Your device is older. Cloud runs on anything with a connection.
  • You generate rarely. A free tier covers occasional use and costs nothing.

Use on-device if…

  • You want to generate a lot. No counter, no queue. Running out is not a thing that happens.
  • You are somewhere without a connection. A plane, a train, a basement, abroad without roaming.
  • The subject matter is private. Photos of your family, work in progress, anything you would rather not upload. It cannot leak from a server it never reached.
  • You dislike subscriptions. On-device generation costs the developer nothing per image, which is the only reason a one-time price is possible at all.

The honest summary of the quality gap

A phone-sized model is roughly a generation behind a server model. That gap is real, and it shows most in complicated prompts, in text, and in hands. It shows least in single subjects, landscapes, textures and mood — which is most of what people actually make.

Where the free built-in option sits

Apple's Image Playground is worth knowing about because it is already on your phone. Since iOS 27 it does photorealistic images as well as illustrated ones, and for occasional use it is free and very good. Two things to know: the photorealistic model runs on Apple's servers, so it needs a connection and comes with usage limits, and the whole feature requires an A17 Pro chip or newer. On older hardware it is not available at all.

That last point catches people out. Some phones that are too old for Apple's own tool can still run a third-party on-device generator, because the memory requirements and the chip requirements are not the same thing.
