Can you generate AI images on an iPhone with no internet?

Yes — with one condition that nobody mentions up front: the model has to get onto your phone first, and it is large. After that, everything happens on the device and the internet is genuinely irrelevant.

← All guides The app

What "on-device" actually means

Most AI image apps are a text box wired to somebody else's computer. You type, your words travel to a server, a graphics card there draws the picture, and it travels back. That is why they meter you — every image costs them real money — and why they stop working on a plane.

An on-device generator puts the model itself on your phone and runs it on Apple's Neural Engine, the chip built for this work. Nothing is sent anywhere. The picture is drawn a few inches from your hand.

The trade you are actually making

You are trading storage and setup time for having no limits and no server.

  • A one-time download of roughly 3 GB. That is the model. It happens once, over Wi-Fi, and never again.
  • A first-launch wait of about two minutes. iOS compiles the model for the Neural Engine the first time. It is unavoidable — Apple provides no way to skip or pre-warm it — and it happens once per install.
  • A modern device. This is the real gate, and it has its own guide.

What you get back: no credit counter, no queue, no account, and it works in a basement, on a flight, or anywhere with no signal at all.

How to check whether it is genuinely offline

Do not take anyone's word for it, including ours. Install the app, let it finish its download, then turn on airplane mode and generate something. If a picture appears, the claim is true. If you get an error, the app was a client for a server the whole time.

What on-device generation is not good at

Being honest about this is more useful than another list of benefits. A phone-sized model is roughly a generation behind what a desktop graphics card runs. It follows long, complicated prompts less precisely, and it will not render text inside an image reliably. If you need a specific paragraph rendered inside a poster, cloud tools are still better at that.

What it is good at is everything else — and doing it without asking permission, without a subscription, and without stopping at image three.
